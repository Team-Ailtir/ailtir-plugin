# Ailtir Contract Playbook — UK General Contractor

This playbook defines standard acceptable commercial positions for UK main contractors under the two dominant standard forms (JCT and NEC4), plus a note on FIDIC where used on UK infrastructure work.

## 1. JCT — Standard Building Contract / Design & Build (2024 editions)

The default private-sector building contract. SBC/Q (with Quantities) and DB (Design and Build) are the most common variants. Amendments are usually captured in a Schedule of Amendments; watch these carefully.

**Standard Positions:**
- **Form:** JCT SBC/Q 2024 (traditional) or JCT DB 2024 (contractor designed). The 2024 edition updates the 2016 wording — most notably around fluctuations, statutory changes, and the JCT-adopted definitions of Insolvency and Force Majeure.
- **Time Bars:** No absolute condition-precedent time bar for Relevant Events. Clause 2.27 (SBC) / 2.24 (DB) requires notice "forthwith" once a delay becomes apparent and the Contractor must give an estimate of expected delay. Practical Completion is defined by the Contract Administrator (or Employer's Agent under DB).
- **Retention:** Standard 3%, released half at Practical Completion and half at Making Good. Higher retention on unamended JCT is a red flag — negotiate down.
- **Liquidated Damages:** Section 2.32 (SBC) / 2.29 (DB). Must be a genuine pre-estimate of loss. Zero LDs is not a defence to unliquidated damages.
- **Weather:** "Exceptionally adverse weather conditions" is a Relevant Event (schedule 2.29.9 / 2.26.8) but not a Relevant Matter — grants time, not money.
- **Payment:** Section 4 — HGCRA-compliant. Interim payment notice = due date + 5 days; pay-less notice = payment date − 5 days.

**What to Flag (Red Risks):**
- Bespoke amendments that convert Relevant Events into Contractor-risk items (weather, statutory undertaker delays, unforeseen ground conditions on D&B).
- Amendments extending payment terms beyond 30 days or removing the Contractor's right to suspend for non-payment (unenforceable under HGCRA s.112 but often attempted).
- Fitness for purpose obligations layered onto a JCT design and build (usually only "reasonable skill and care" is contemplated — fitness for purpose voids most PI cover).
- Design liability layered on the Contractor under SBC/Q when the Employer's design is being novated — split points must be crystal clear.
- Programme dates that assume no float, or make Practical Completion a condition precedent to payment.

## 2. NEC4 — Engineering and Construction Contract (ECC)

The default public-sector infrastructure and buildings contract in the UK, mandated by Cabinet Office guidance for most central government works and used widely by highways, rail, water, and health authorities.

**Standard Positions:**
- **Form:** NEC4 ECC Option A (priced with activity schedule), C (target cost with pain/gain share), or E (cost reimbursable). Option A is the closest analogue to a JCT lump sum.
- **Time Bars:** **Strict 8-week bar on Compensation Events (CEs).** Clause 61.3 — if the Contractor does not notify a CE within 8 weeks of becoming aware, entitlement to time and money is lost, unless the Project Manager should have notified it themselves (61.1).
- **Early Warnings:** Clause 15 — mandatory notice by either party of anything that could increase cost, delay Completion, delay a Key Date, or impair performance. Not itself a claim, but failure to notify affects assessment of a later CE (clause 61.5).
- **Compensation Event quotations:** 3 weeks to submit a quotation after instruction (clause 62.3); Project Manager has 2 weeks to respond (62.3). Missed deadlines can trigger "deemed acceptance" (clause 62.6 — but read the specific option's Z-clauses; some UK employers delete this).
- **Retention:** Not part of the core clauses. If present, it is added as Secondary Option X16 — commonly 3–5%.
- **Delay Damages:** Secondary Option X7. Must be a genuine pre-estimate; zero X7 = unliquidated damages remain available.
- **Programme:** Clause 31/32 — Accepted Programme is a live document, revised at defined intervals. Programme not accepted → 25% of amount otherwise due can be retained (clause 50.5 — mind Z-clause deletions).

**What to Flag (Red Risks):**
- Z-clauses that delete or dilute clause 15 (early warnings), 61 (CE notification), or 63 (CE assessment). Common on employer-heavy amendments.
- Z-clauses adding fitness for purpose or extending liability beyond the standard NEC4 limits.
- Aggressive Key Date regimes with delay damages attached — check X5 (Sectional Completion) and X7 interaction.
- Option C target-cost projects with a pain share exceeding 50% and no gain share — asymmetric risk.
- Deletion of the "Contractor's share" cap in Option C.
- No Named Suppliers under Option A (X10 not adopted) — check subcontractor approval process.

## 3. FIDIC — International contracts on UK infrastructure

Occasionally used on UK energy, water, or joint-venture infrastructure work where an international EPC contractor is involved. Rarely the primary form on UK public works but appears as a subcontract or joint-venture instrument.

**Standard Positions:**
- **Form:** FIDIC Red Book 2017 (Employer-designed), Yellow Book 2017 (Contractor-designed / Plant), Silver Book 2017 (EPC / turnkey).
- **Time Bars:** Sub-clause 20.2 — Contractor's Claim notice within **28 days** of becoming aware of the event. Full particulars within 84 days. Missing the 28-day bar = loss of entitlement (though modified 2017 wording gives the Engineer some discretion).
- **DAB / DAAB:** Dispute Avoidance/Adjudication Board is a condition precedent to arbitration. Standing board under 2017 forms.
- **Retention:** Typically 5%, released against Taking-Over Certificate and Performance Certificate.

**What to Flag (Red Risks):**
- Under Silver Book, the Contractor takes fitness-for-purpose risk and unforeseen ground conditions — verify PI cover extends this far.
- 2017 forms introduce advance-warning obligations (sub-clause 8.4) and a much more prescribed claim-notification process — small procedural slips forfeit claims.

## 4. Sub-contract equivalents (for reference when acting up-tier)

- Under JCT: JCT SBCSub or JCT DBSub — mirror pay-when-certified prohibitions of HGCRA.
- Under NEC4: NEC4 Sub-Contract (ECS) — same time-bar and Early Warning regime as ECC.

## 5. Statutory overlay — always applies

- **HGCRA 1996 (as amended by LDEDCA 2009):** Right to interim payment, right to adjudicate any time, right to suspend for non-payment (subject to 7 days' notice), prohibition on pay-when-paid except upstream insolvency.
- **Construction Act payment mechanism:** Payment due date → payment notice (5 days after) → pay-less notice (usually 5 days before final date) → final date for payment. Any bespoke mechanism that fails HGCRA s.110/111 tests is void and the Scheme for Construction Contracts applies.
- **Building Safety Act 2022:** Higher-Risk Buildings (residential ≥18m or ≥7 storeys with ≥2 dwellings) trigger Gateway 2/3 duties, dutyholder competence obligations, and the "golden thread" of information. Where a project falls under HRB scope, contract review must flag which party bears BSA competency and information-transfer risk.
- **CDM 2015:** Principal Designer / Principal Contractor duties are statutory, not contractual — they exist regardless of the contract form.
